# Water 7 — Research Notes

Source material: One Piece **Water 7 Arc** — manga ch. 322–374 (Water 7 proper; the
wider Water 7 Saga runs to ch. 441), anime ep. 229–263 (Water 7) continuing through
the Enies Lobby departure. Cross-checked against the supplied reference stills, which
are treated as the **primary source of truth** for proportion, colour and layout.

---

## 1. Island form — the single most important fact

Water 7 is **not a flat Venice**. It is a **stepped cone**: a roughly circular island
that rises in concentric terraces to a summit, described in-universe as looking like
"a giant fountain in a volcano shape."

The shape has an in-world cause worth preserving, because it drives everything else:
the island is **slowly sinking**. Each generation built new houses on top of the older,
drowned ones. So the city is a vertical accumulation of urban layers — the lowest ring
is the newest-but-most-doomed, and the oldest streets are underwater beneath it.

Confirmed by references:
- `Water_7_Infobox.webp` — the island as a blue-white stepped pyramid, tall spire
  fountain at the summit, **numbered dock arches ("2", "3") at the base ring**.
- `hq720 (2).jpg` — the island from sea level: a terraced mound, **waterfalls pouring
  out of arched openings all around the base**, blue dome at the peak.
- `Water_7_on_dry_land.webp` — the same mound with the sea withdrawn, showing the
  terraces as solid built-up mass sitting on a green base.

## 2. The Great Fountain and the water system

A colossal tiered fountain crowns the summit. Water flows **down and outward** from it
through the city: radial canals cascade from terrace to terrace and finally spill into
the sea through arched water-gates in the base ring. This is why the base is ringed
with waterfalls in `hq720`.

Consequence for the build: canals are not one flat water level. Each terrace has its
own water level, and the radial canals are a **staircase of pools and falls**.

## 3. Canals as streets

Canals replace roads. Residents travel by boat, most characteristically **Yagara Bulls**
(seahorse-like mounts) towing gondola-carts. Wide canals admit full-size ships and lead
them to dockable berths inside the city. Arched stone footbridges cross the narrower
ones. `images (16).jpeg` and `random-rumble` show the signature view: a teal-green canal
street walled by tall cream buildings, small boats, arched bridges overhead.

## 4. The seven docks — Galley-La Company

Water 7 historically had **seven dry docks**, each run by a separate company. Iceburg,
a student of Tom, united all seven into the **Galley-La Company**. Each dock is entered
through a **towering door marked with its number**, behind which the shipyard sits.
Each has five foremen.

- **Dock 1** — the flagship. Best shipwrights, best ships; its foremen are treated as
  celebrities by the townspeople. The only dock shown in any detail
  (`Dock_1_Infobox.webp`: a heavy dark gantry crane over a drained dry dock, timber
  stacked on the floor, red-roofed sheds crowding the rim).
- **Docks 2–7** — never depicted in the series. They are *named* but not *designed*,
  so we extrapolate from Dock 1 with varied size and equipment. This is stated
  explicitly in the layout plan so it is not mistaken for canon.

## 5. Galley-La HQ / Iceburg's mansion

Combined shipwright headquarters and **mayoral** residence — Iceburg is both company
president and mayor of Water 7. It is the **largest home on the island** and heavily
guarded. Two distinct on-screen buildings exist and both are referenced:
- the tall pale stone block with a round oculus window and signage
  (`Galley-La_Company_Info...`),
- the broad blue-violet timber hall with `GALLEY-LA COMPANY` painted across the
  facade, standing on grass (`Second_Galley-La_Headq...`).

## 6. Franky House and Scrap Island

- **Franky House** — the Franky Family's base on the **outskirts**, scrap-built, with
  `FRANKY HOUSE` in large gold letters and **large robot-like arms hanging off its
  sides**.
- **Scrap Island** — a man-made offshore heap of debris from hundreds of wrecked and
  scrapped ships. Later the build site of the Thousand Sunny.

## 7. Blue Station and the Sea Train

**Blue Station** is the Sea Train terminus, where the *Puffing Tom* departs across the
open water on raised rails. It is the island's link to the outside world and becomes
desperately crowded when Aqua Laguna approaches and citizens flee.

## 8. Aqua Laguna

An annual storm surge that swallows the **lower city** under a huge wave. Every year
the population evacuates the low ring for higher ground. This confirms the terraces are
a real, functional elevation hierarchy — the base ring is expendable, the upper city is
refuge. We encode this as an explicit "flood line" in the layout even though the
current build is set on a calm afternoon.

## 9. Visual language (from the references, above any written description)

| Element | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Walls | Sun-bleached cream / warm peach plaster over stone |
| Roofs | **Red terracotta half-barrel vaults** — the signature form |
| Roof detail | Concentric lighter-red / salmon arcs banding the barrel's gable end |
| Towers | Cylindrical, capped with red hemispherical domes |
| Windows | Small, square-ish, white surrounds, **lavender-tinted glass** |
| Canal walls | Grey ashlar block, lion / gargoyle spout heads at the waterline |
| Retaining walls | Massive rough grey blocks between terraces (`random-rumble-v0`) |
| Water | Blue-green to teal, bright and calm |
| Light | Warm late-afternoon sun, long soft shadows |

The barrel-vault roof with concentric arcs is the one detail that makes a silhouette
read as Water 7 instantly. It is worth more than any other single piece of modelling.
